01 — Brand Positioning

Where We
Operate

01

The Audience

Urban professionals, 22–40, London. High-output people who treat their body as infrastructure — and their lunch break as the hinge between morning and afternoon performance.

02

The Product

Premium poke bowls. Above-average price point, justified by quality, freshness, and a bold visual identity. Fast but not fast-food.

03

The Space

Premium fast-casual. Competing not just with poke chains but with any brand that touches London's wellness, performance, and lifestyle culture.

Key Insights

What we know
about our people

01 Energy is the real product +
People don't come for food. They come to feel sharp, light, and back in motion. The bowl is the delivery mechanism for a feeling.
02 The lunch hour is the pivot point +
For white-collar London, 1PM is the hinge of the day. What you eat there either fuels or drags the afternoon.
03 Premium ≠ slow or joyless +
This audience rejects trade-offs. They want quality, speed, and pleasure — simultaneously. Honi actually delivers that.
04 Stories move people, facts don't +
A vivid bowl is forgotten. A bowl that belongs to a story — a pitch, a first date, a run, a reunion — lives in memory and gets shared.
The Positioning Idea
Charged.

Honi Poke is the fuel for people who don't stop — and the place where their stories happen. Every great day has a Honi in it somewhere.

Key Messages

Three ideas
we say loud

01 Fuel that keeps up with you +
For the runner, the founder, the creative director eating at their desk. Honi Poke matches your pace — told through real people, real moments.

Every lunch break is a scene in someone's bigger story. The people who perform best in the afternoon made a better choice at lunch.
03 Colour is a promise +
What looks energetic, is energetic. The bold aesthetic of Honi Poke isn't decoration — it signals freshness, intensity, and life.

Why Now · Trend Context

Three forces
aligning

01 Swicy is the flavour of 2026 +
Sweet-heat is the #1 flavour trend of 2026, named by National Geographic, Tastewise and FlavourTrends as the defining combination of the year. McDonald's, Wingstop, Hershey and Takis all made structural commitments to swicy in Q1 — this is no longer a trend, it's an industry framework.
02 Protein culture is mainstream +
Post-pandemic wellness has made protein consciousness universal, not niche. Our audience expects it. More protein options make the choice easier.
03 The premium salad gap +
London lacks a fast-casual brand owning the premium salad space with real flavour credibility. Poke Salads fills that gap with Honi's existing DNA.

06 — 1PM Strategy

Own the
hour.

Life happens every minute. We don't sell only at 1PM, and we don't pretend we do. But in a customer's head, we want one hour to belong to us — the way certain brands own a colour or a sound. 1PM is the wrapper, not the cage.

Own the hour in the mind.

Sell across the day in reality.

What 1PM does for the brand

Three reasons to commit
01

A Centre of Gravity

It gives the storytelling a centre of gravity. A specific hour is more memorable than a vague feeling, and it lets us say something distinctive in a category where everyone says the same things about freshness, quality, and speed.

02

A Ritual to Opt Into

It gives customers a ritual to opt into. People love belonging to a moment that's bigger than them.

03

It Leaves Room

Because 1PM is a wrapper, not a rule, we can run dinner messaging, breakfast bowls, late-afternoon promotions, weekend campaigns — and the brand stays consistent. The hour is the signature; the menu is the business.

The Five Tools

How the strategy lives
01Tool One

The 1PM Ritual

The umbrella. Everything emotional about the strategy lives here. The voice is specific, restrained, never explaining. The bowl appears as a fact, not a hero.

Packaging A one-sentence story printed on the bowl, bag, pots, napkins, labels. Dozens of stories in rotation. Each one signed off with Life, in a bowl. In-store Story Walls, rotating quarterly. Real stories from real customers in that exact branch, plus editorial stories from the brand's own writing. Social The same stories, formatted vertical, rolling out across Instagram and TikTok. Some shot, some typographic, some animated.
“He said it was just lunch. It wasn't.” “Three meetings, two trains, one dreaded call, and the first proper breath of the day came at one o'clock.” “Eleven years of Thursday lunches. The first one alone, she ordered his usual.”
02Tool Two

The 1PM Playlist

The in-store ritual. Every Honi branch plays The 1PM Playlist between 12:45 and 1:30. Curated weekly, leans into mood and motion, lives on Spotify so customers can take it with them.

Why The store experience is sharper, the playlist becomes a Honi-owned cultural artifact, and the customer associates a specific sound with the hour. Free, repeatable, distinctive. Counter Card A small printed card on the counter: The 1PM Playlist. Live now. Spotify: Honi Poke.
03Tool Three

Share Your 1PM

The UGC engine. Crucially not share your Honi story — that locks people into the brand. Instead we open it up: share your 1PM. Wider, more human, far more shareable. We're not asking customers to advertise us. We're asking them to belong to a ritual they can claim.

Prompts Quarterly Story sticker and DM prompts: What happened at 1PM today? · Show us your 1PM ritual. · Your 1PM, in three words. Activation The best submissions become poster art at the originating branch — printed at the exact location where the story happened. Customer walks past, sees their own words on the wall. The Asset This is the slow, compounding asset. Q1 builds the library. By Q3, the brand has a living archive of London at 1PM.
04Tool Four

The 1PM Ritual Archive

The Story Index. A web destination at honipoke.com/1pm. Every story ever told, searchable by neighbourhood, mood, and territory. Customers can submit directly. Linked from every packaging item and poster via a small printed URL.

What it becomes The brand's quiet cultural archive. Press write about it. Customers send it to friends. Other brands reference it. Why it matters The Archive isn't a campaign — it's the proof that the strategy is real.
05Tool Five

The 1PM Show

The social show. Weekly Tuesday drop. London office workers, comedic escalation, bowl appearing in the final beat. This is where the brand is loudest about owning the hour.

Selling outside 1PM

The playful counter-line

Because the strategy is a wrapper, not a cage, we get to break it on purpose. This is what makes it human.

“It's never too late for your 1PM bowl.” 5PM · Social
“It's barely 11. Some people start their 1PM early.” Mornings
“1PM is a state of mind.” Any time
“Friday afternoons don't count. Order anyway.” Weekends

These run in scheduled slots outside the lunch window — late afternoon promotions, weekend social, evening delivery campaigns. They reinforce the cornerstone by acknowledging it. The brand has a sense of humour about its own claim.

Why this works

Four reasons it sticks

It's distinctive

No other London food brand owns a time. Pret owns coffee, Itsu owns Asian fast-casual, Leon owns naturally-fast-food. The lunch hour itself is unclaimed.

It's defensible without being defensive

We don't have to fight for the hour. We just have to keep telling stories inside it, and competitors will sound late by default.

It compounds

The Archive grows. The Story Walls rotate. By year two, customers can't separate the hour from the brand.

It leaves us free

Dinner, breakfast, weekends, delivery — all available, all on-brand, because the cornerstone is a wrapper, not a rule.

It's true

The lunch hour really is when life happens for our audience. We're not inventing the connection. We're amplifying one that's already there.

Charged energy. Human stories. London life, in a bowl.
